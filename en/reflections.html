REFLECTIONS

Your own Shinkansen, in the glass

You can't see the train you're riding from its own window. But for a few seconds, it can appear in the glass of trackside buildings and factories.

Hansha-tetsu: reflection shots from the window

Photo: Shinkansen Window (Osaki Garden Tower)　(c)Disney

WHAT IS IT

Glass is a mirror outside the window

The blue train running in a line across the lower glass is the one the photographer was riding. Japanese rail fans call this hansha-tetsu (反射鉄): a train reflection shot of your own train, caught in the glass of buildings right beside the tracks. You ride the train and photograph it at the same time, and we think that is a big part of the fun. Here are the places on the Tokaido Shinkansen where it has worked for us, with the side of the train to look from.

Note: the daytime photos on this page all show the Disney-themed Sparkling Dreams Shinkansen. For another English introduction to hansha-tetsu, see Tokyo Railway Labyrinth ↗.

TWO KINDS

Two kinds of places

Buildings near stations

Yurakucho, Tokyo–Shinagawa, past Shinagawa, Shin-Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto

Around departures and arrivals the train slows to about 60–70 km/h (at Shin-Yokohama it was already accelerating, at about 160 km/h). Glassy buildings stand right by the tracks; past Shinagawa, the train appeared in two of them within about 13 seconds.

Factories and company sites

POLA Fukuroi Factory, FUJITEC

Long walls of glass parallel to the tracks, with open ground in front. Even at 150–250 km/h the train shows up end to end. It only lasts a moment, so it helps to know where to look.

POLA Chemical Industries, Fukuroi Factory

End to end in the long glass

Past Kakegawa, around Fukuroi, a long white factory appears on the Seat A side. Beyond the lawn and hedges, the train runs end to end along the bottom of its glass. At about 250 km/h, it is over in a moment.

Toward Shin-Osaka, the Otsuka Pharmaceutical factory (with the POCARI SWEAT lettering) follows on the same side about 10 seconds later. Toward Tokyo, look about 10 seconds after passing it.

Either side of Nagoya Station

Just before arriving, and just after leaving

Around Nagoya Station, glassy buildings line the tracks on the Seat A side. Just before arriving, a building with tall windows reflects the train as it slows down, and the Meitetsu (Nagoya Railroad) sign from across the tracks shows up mirrored near the top of the glass. Long glass beside the research tower

HOW TO

Filming it yourself

  1. Shoot video. Watching the train glide through the glass is more fun on video than in a photo, and you can pull a still from it later. Many of the photos here are frames from video.
  2. Hold the lens close to the window to cut down reflections from inside the car.
  3. You don't need sunshine. Every photo on this page was taken on a cloudy day.
  4. Film from your own seat. Keep aisles and vestibules clear, and avoid capturing people or rooms inside the buildings.
