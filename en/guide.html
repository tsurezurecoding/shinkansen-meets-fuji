When can you see Mt. Fuji from the bullet train — and which side?
— Right side heading to Kyoto, left heading back to Tokyo. Seat E both ways, across all 46 window views.

Heading to Kyoto, Mt. Fuji is on the right. Heading back to Tokyo, it is on the left. Either way the seat is Seat E.

Mt. Fuji sits north of the track, so the side flips with your direction — but seat letters do not. Green Car: Seat D.

Which side, and how to book Seat E

  1. Pick any train — including with a Japan Rail Pass. Nozomi, Hikari and Kodama all run on the same track, so the Fuji view is identical. A Japan Rail Pass covers Hikari and Kodama at no extra charge, and Nozomi requires a separate special ticket for pass holders. Seat E is the Fuji-side window position in ordinary cars. With a reserved-seat ticket you can choose it in advance; a Japan Rail Pass includes seat reservations on eligible Hikari and Kodama services at no additional charge. Riding on a pass? Mt. Fuji times for Hikari and Kodama are listed separately, because they are not the Nozomi times quoted everywhere. For current fares and pass conditions, check the official sources: smartEX (JR Central official booking) and japanrailpass.net. Green Car note: the Fuji-side window there is Seat D.
  2. Choose “E” on the seat map — ticket machine, JR office, or the smartEX app. It's the window seat of the two-seat row. Seats rotate at the terminus, so E faces Fuji in both directions. Couples: book D+E.
  3. If Seat E is sold out, do not force a better view. Only use another window briefly when the carriage is quiet and you can do so without blocking aisles or vestibules. Otherwise, aim for Left-side Fuji from Seat A or Lake Hamana, which works from both sides.

Your three chances at Fuji

~27 minSeat E 1 · The preview Fuji rises grandly beyond the wide plain and layered Tanzawa ridges. Winter mornings are especially crisp. ~43 minSeat E 2 · The main event Bigger after every tunnel until it fills the window. 3–4 minutes. ~54 minSeat A 3 · The lucky left Fuji A curve throws Fuji to the opposite side for ~30 seconds. Edo travelers prized it too.

Even before these three, on very clear days you may catch a small Fuji beyond the city from around Ota. It feels like an early signal just after leaving Tokyo.

On exceptional winter days there is a fourth: Fuji floating far beyond Lake Hamana (~73 min). If you catch all of them in one ride, you have done something most Japanese passengers never have.

Shinkansen window views still work on cloudy days

If the forecast is cloudy on the day you plan to see Mt. Fuji from the Shinkansen, the whole window journey is not lost. Lakes, nearby mountains, castles and tea fields depend far less on long-distance visibility, and some colors and outlines become richer under grey skies.

~73 minboth sides Lake Hamana The train skims the water on a low bridge — grey sky just makes it moodier. ~110 minSeat E Mt. Ibuki Close and massive — it holds its shape under any sky. Snow-capped in winter. ~62 minSeat E Kakegawa Castle A wooden castle keep right by the station — near enough to beat the haze.

And that's just three. Sagami Bay, tea fields, seven castles, Kyoto's pagoda — browse all 46 views in the window gallery. A grey sky costs you one of them.

Shinkansen window views include night scenery too

It is a mistake to assume there is nothing outside after dark. City skylines, working port lights, illuminated castles and a floodlit train depot create a different sequence of views from the daytime ride.

~3 minSeat E Tokyo Tower, lit Orange against the night sky, minutes after departure. ~50 minSeat A Shimizu port at night Cranes and ships in work lights — an industrial constellation on the dark bay. ~99 minSeat E Kiyosu Castle light-up Nobunaga's castle glows right beside the track.

More night views include the Musashi-Kosugi skyline, Atami's lights along the coast, the towers around Nagoya Station, and Torikai Shinkansen Depot. Browse only night views →

After dark the window becomes a mirror. Move away from cabin lights, press closer to the glass and cup your hands around your eyes.
