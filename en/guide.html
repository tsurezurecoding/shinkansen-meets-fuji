Mt. Fuji from the Shinkansen:
which side, what time, best seat

Book Seat E: here is how

Ordinary cars on the Tokaido Shinkansen have three seats (A, B, C) on one side of the aisle and two seats (D, E) on the other. Mt. Fuji appears on the two-seat side, at window Seat E, the right side of the train when traveling from Tokyo toward Kyoto and Osaka. Seats are rotated at the terminus, so it is also Seat E traveling toward Tokyo. You never need to remember left or right: just book E.

When reserving, at a ticket machine, at a JR office, or in the smartEX app, pick your seat from the seat map and choose any seat marked "E". Seat D (the aisle seat next to it) still gets a good view, so couples should book D and E together. If Seat E is sold out, do not give up: walk to the vestibule a few minutes before the viewing section and enjoy an unobstructed window.

Traveling with a Japan Rail Pass? Hikari and Kodama trains have the same view and do not need a Nozomi/Mizuho supplement. Nozomi is available to JR Pass holders only with the special Nozomi Mizuho ticket purchased before boarding. In the Green Car (2+2 layout), the Fuji-side window is Seat D.

You get more than one chance

1. Across the Sagami Plain (25-27 min from Tokyo, Seat E)

On clear days, especially winter mornings, Mt. Fuji appears in the distance beyond the plain between Shin-Yokohama and Odawara. Consider it a preview.

2. The main event: Mishima → Shin-Fuji (about 43 min, Seat E)

The mountain grows bigger after each tunnel until it fills the window, foothills and all, for about 3-4 minutes. It draws an audible gasp from the car every single time.

3. Left Fuji (about 54 min, Seat A, about 28 seconds)

Just after Shin-Fuji, a curve in the track briefly puts Mt. Fuji on the opposite side: Seat A. It lasts less than half a minute, and almost nobody knows about it. Travelers on the old Tokaido road treasured the same phenomenon 300 years ago. Details on the Left Fuji page.

On exceptional days you can even spot Mt. Fuji across Lake Hamana (about 73 min), far to the west.

Raise your odds: season and time of day

Winter is by far the best season. From November to February the air is clear, the summit is snow-capped, and the mountain is visible most days. In summer (June-August) it hides behind haze and clouds more often than not, so set expectations accordingly. Mornings beat afternoons: clouds tend to build around the summit as the day warms up. If you can choose, take a morning train in winter and the odds are firmly on your side.

Checking a Mt. Fuji live camera or the weather forecast the evening before will tell you what to expect. Even on cloudy days the foothills often show, so Seat E is never wasted.

Photo tips at 285 km/h

The one rule: be ready five minutes early. The train is fast, and if you reach for your camera when you first see the mountain, the best framing is already gone. When the train passes Mishima, camera up.

Press your lens close to the window glass to reduce cabin reflections. Buildings and poles near the track will streak through the frame, so use burst mode, or simply record video and pull frames later. Mt. Fuji itself is far away and stays sharp; a phone camera does the job well.

Mt. Fuji is just the beginning

The Tokaido Shinkansen window keeps going: Sagami Bay and the resort town of Atami, the train skimming across Lake Hamana, Odawara, Hikone and Gifu castles, tea fields, and the five-story pagoda of Toji Temple announcing your arrival in Kyoto. The window view guide lists every field-tested highlight with photos, and once you pick your train, every one of them gets a passing time.

FAQ

Which side of the Shinkansen is Mt. Fuji on?

Seat E, the window seat of the two-seat row. That is the right side traveling from Tokyo, and it stays Seat E in both directions because seats are rotated at the terminus.

How long after Tokyo?

About 40-45 minutes on a Nozomi, between Mishima and Shin-Fuji. Hikari and Kodama take longer; the app shows the exact time for your train.

Coming from Kyoto or Osaka?

Roughly 1 hour 40 minutes after Shin-Osaka on a Nozomi (about 1 hour 25 minutes after Kyoto). Same side: Seat E.

How long is it visible?

About 3-4 minutes at the main section, interrupted by tunnels; it grows bigger after each one.

Does this work with the JR Pass?

Yes. Hikari and Kodama trains have the identical view. Nozomi requires the special Nozomi Mizuho ticket for JR Pass holders.

What if it is cloudy?

It happens, especially in summer. But the window has castles, lakes, the sea, and Kyoto's Toji pagoda in store; the app shows what is coming next on your train.
