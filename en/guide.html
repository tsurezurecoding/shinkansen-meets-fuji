Booking Seat E, in three steps

  1. Pick any train. Nozomi, Hikari and Kodama all pass Fuji the same way — the view is identical. Seat E is a reserved seat, and reserved seats carry a fee on every train type (fares differ slightly between train types; non-reserved cars are cheaper but you can't pick a seat). Japan Rail Pass rules differ by train type too. For current fares and pass conditions, check the official sources: smartEX (JR Central official booking) and japanrailpass.net. Green Car note: the Fuji-side window there is Seat D.
  2. Choose “E” on the seat map — ticket machine, JR office, or the smartEX app. It's the window seat of the two-seat row. Seats rotate at the terminus, so E faces Fuji in both directions. Couples: book D+E.
  3. E sold out? Stand in the vestibule window a few minutes before Mishima. Nobody will mind for three minutes.

Your three chances at Fuji

~27 minSeat E 1 · The preview Across the Sagami Plain, small and far. Clear winter mornings only. ~43 minSeat E 2 · The main event Bigger after every tunnel until it fills the window. 3–4 minutes. ~54 minSeat A 3 · The lucky left Fuji A curve throws Fuji to the opposite side for ~30 seconds. Edo travelers prized it too.

Even before these three, on very clear days you may catch a small Fuji beyond the city from around Ota. It feels like an early signal just after leaving Tokyo.

On exceptional winter days there is a fourth: Fuji floating far beyond Lake Hamana (~73 min). If you catch all of them in one ride, you have done something most Japanese passengers never have.

Cloudy? The window doesn't care.

Fuji hides behind haze roughly half the year (summer especially — winter mornings are your best odds). But most of this route's views work in any weather. These never cancel:

~73 minboth sides Lake Hamana The train skims the water on a low bridge — grey sky just makes it moodier. ~110 minSeat E Mt. Ibuki Close and massive — it holds its shape under any sky. Snow-capped in winter. ~62 minSeat E Kakegawa Castle A wooden castle keep right by the station — near enough to beat the haze.

And that's just three. Sagami Bay, tea fields, seven castles, Kyoto's pagoda — browse all 37 views in the window gallery. A grey sky costs you one of them.

Riding after dark

Night trains hide the scenery — but not everything. If you're on a late departure, look for these lights:

~3 minSeat E Tokyo Tower, lit Orange against the night sky, minutes after departure. ~50 minSeat A Shimizu port at night Cranes and ships in work lights — an industrial constellation on the dark bay. ~99 minSeat E Kiyosu Castle light-up Nobunaga's castle glows right beside the track.

There are more lights along the line — see the night views in the window gallery. One tip: the window turns into a mirror after dark, so press close to the glass and cup your hands.

FAQ

Which side is Mt. Fuji on?

Seat E — the two-seat side. Right side from Tokyo, left side toward Tokyo, but always Seat E.

How long after Tokyo?

40–45 min on a Nozomi, between Mishima and Shin-Fuji. Longer on Hikari/Kodama — the timeline shows your exact train.

From Kyoto or Osaka?

About 1h40m after Shin-Osaka (1h25m after Kyoto). Same seat: E.

Do reserved seats cost extra?

Yes, on every train type — not just Nozomi. Exact fares and Japan Rail Pass conditions change, so check smartEX or japanrailpass.net when you book.

What if it's cloudy?

You lose one view out of thirty-six. The lake, the big mountain, the trackside castle and the Kyoto pagoda don't need a clear sky.

Anything at night?

Tokyo Tower, the port lights of Shimizu, Kiyosu Castle's light-up — the gallery has a night filter.
