A SMALL DISCOVERY, BEFORE OR AFTER THE TRAIN

The journey starts with a closer look.

A station can be something to enjoy in its own right. Tokyo has creatures above the gates and a roof you can recognise from the train. Kyoto opens into a landscape of steel, glass and steps.

You can enjoy these details after arriving, before leaving, or on a city walk. Start with whichever catches your eye.

01 / 東京駅 / TOKYO · LOOK UP

Animals above the ticket gates.

Before looking for a platform, give the dome a second look. Its grand ceiling is full of smaller things to find.

Marunouchi North or South entrance hall · west side · outside the ticket gates

Eight animals. Twelve signs.

Japan’s zodiac has twelve animal signs. Eight appear around these domes, placed according to the directions traditionally associated with them. The rat, rabbit, horse and rooster are absent.

The large white birds are eagles. They are a separate part of the decoration, not the eight zodiac animals.

TRY A SECOND LOOK

Find an eagle. Then look for a smaller white creature against green. The whole ceiling starts to feel like a collection of living things.

The official story of the domes ↗

Finding the domes

Use the ground-floor Marunouchi North or South entrance hall, outside the JR gates. The Tokaido Shinkansen is on the opposite, Yaesu side. Keep entrances and walking routes clear when looking up.

Visit before entering the paid area or after arriving. If you have already entered, ask staff about your ticket before leaving the gates.

02 / 東京駅 / TOKYO · LOOK AGAIN

The red-brick station, twice.

See the front from Marunouchi. Then see whether you can find that roof again, beyond the platforms.

01 / ON FOOT

Learn the shape of the roof.

A survivor, carefully brought back.

Tokyo Station opened in 1914. Its three-storey outline was restored in 2012. The brick structure of the first two floors was preserved, while the third floor was rebuilt. What looks like one old building carries both preservation and reconstruction.

Look at the domes and their dark roofs. Those shapes will help you recognise the station from a very different angle.

THE SAME STATION, A DIFFERENT VIEW

A familiar roof in a moving city.

The dark dome and red brick appear behind tracks, platforms and wires. Look just left of the centre of this photograph. The station looks much smaller against Marunouchi’s high-rises.

Look toward the E-seat side just after leaving Tokyo on the Tokaido Shinkansen, or just before arriving. This is a glimpse through railway structures, so the view changes with the train and your position.

Tokyo Station, Tokyo Tower & the city from the train →
Finding the Marunouchi front

The public forecourt is outside the Marunouchi exits on the west side. Enjoy the front from ground level; a hotel or rooftop visit is not needed. The Shinkansen gates are on the Yaesu side, so allow time to find your way across the station.

03 / 京都駅 / KYOTO · LOOK AROUND

A station you can step into.

Kyoto’s old streets meet a thoroughly modern gateway. Inside, the architecture feels almost like a landscape.

Central Entrance, Karasuma side · north side · outside the ticket gates

A valley in steel and glass.

Architect Hiroshi Hara imagined the central concourse as a valley, with terraces rising to the east and west. The building’s plan also draws on the grid of ancient Kyoto’s streets. The connection with the old city goes deeper than the surface.

TRY A SECOND LOOK

From the public concourse by the Central Entrance, follow the rising levels with your eyes. You can enjoy the shape without heading all the way to the roof.

Kyoto Station Building’s design concept (Japanese) ↗

A PLACE TO STAY, AS WELL AS PASS THROUGH

When the steps become seats.

The Grand Staircase has 171 steps. At its foot is the fourth-floor Muromachi-koji Square. During performances, the stairs can become audience seating: a route through the building turns into a place to pause.

From the square, notice how wide the steps are. You do not have to climb them all to appreciate the idea.

Official square and staircase guide (Japanese) ↗

ANOTHER VIEW / SKYWAY

A different way to see the space.

If you want to explore further, the tenth-floor Skyway crosses above the concourse and looks out toward the city. The official guide currently lists access from 10:00 to 22:00.

Check the official Skyway guide (Japanese) ↗
Finding the spaces & planning a visit

The public concourse and these station-building spaces are on the Karasuma (north) side. The Shinkansen is towards Hachijo (south). Outside the rail gates, follow signs for the north–south public passage and Central Entrance.

Muromachi-koji Square and the staircase are listed as open 06:00–23:00. The official access note lists the JR Kyoto Isetan lift to the fourth-floor square as available 10:00–20:00. Confirm a suitable lift route with staff; those hours differ from the square’s hours. Events can change access.
