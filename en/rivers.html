Rivers and bridges from Tokyo to Shin-Osaka

A different view at every river

Look for the stream winding through the gravel, or the bridge beside your own. Each crossing gives you something new to look for.

Photography by Shinkansen Window · Fuji River

In order from Tokyo to Shin-Osaka Select a photo to enlarge it.

01

Shinagawa – Shin-Yokohama E-seat side

Tama RiverMaruko Bridge

The city opens up and the pale blue arches of Maruko Bridge come into view. As you cross by Shinkansen, people on the road bridge are crossing the same river on their daily rounds.

A closer look at Maruko Bridge →
02

Shin-Yokohama – Odawara E-seat side

Sagami RiverThe Sakawa and the old Tokaido

Between the grey diagonal bridge beams, a broad stretch of water and green banks on both sides come into view. On the old Tokaido, travelers crossed the Sagami by boat; near Odawara, porters carried travelers across the Sakawa.

Look for Mount Fuji across the Sagami Plain →
03

Shin-Yokohama – Odawara E-seat side

Sakawa River

Near Odawara, look upstream from the E-seat side for the gates of the Iizumi Intake Weir. Water drawn here supplies homes in Kanagawa Prefecture.

The pharmaceutical plant by the Sakawa →
04

Odawara – Atami A-seat side

Shiraito River BridgeA valley opening onto the sea

Near Nebukawa, look for the sea beyond a line of red bridge piers. The bridge in the window carries the conventional Tokaido Main Line. Its tall piers give a sense of the valley's depth even when the water below is out of sight.

See the Shiraito River Bridge →
05

Shin-Fuji – Shizuoka E-seat side

Fuji RiverA water pipeline and Mount Fuji

The white arches in front of Mount Fuji carry a pipeline supplying water to factories. In a single view, water takes two routes, one along the riverbed and another across the bridge.

Explore the Fuji River and its pipeline bridge →
06

Shizuoka – Kakegawa

Abe River

Follow the slender stream as it bends across a wide gravel bed. The distance your train travels on the bridge can be much greater than the width of the flowing water below.

07

Shizuoka – Kakegawa

Oi River

Look between the bridge girders for a channel sweeping around the gravel. Travelers on the old Tokaido road relied on river porters to get across. Your train covers the same river in moments.

08

Kakegawa – Hamamatsu

Tenryu River

Patches of green grow on the stony riverbed, while the water follows its edge. Compare this with the Abe and Oi. All three have expanses of gravel, but the stream and the greenery occupy different parts of the view.

The Abe, Oi and Tenryu photos were taken on different days and facing different directions. Rain and water levels reshape the channels. These three views alone cannot tell us which river usually carries the most water.

09

Toyohashi – Mikawa-Anjo

Toyo RiverThe main channel and floodway

Just beyond Toyohashi, the broad main river comes into view alongside a path on the embankment. Going toward Shin-Osaka, watch for another crossing about 1.6 km later. This is the Toyo floodway, which diverts high water from the main river into Mikawa Bay.

10

Toyohashi – Mikawa-Anjo A-seat side

Toyogawa Floodway

Heading from Toyohashi toward Shin-Osaka, you cross the main Toyo River first and the floodway next. From the A-seat side, its straight banks lead toward the evening sun. Toward Tokyo, the floodway comes first.

Toyohashi – Mikawa-Anjo

Yahagi River

On the way to Mikawa-Anjo, the train crosses the Yahagi River. Water drawn farther upstream reaches the fields of Anjo through the Meiji irrigation canal. The river below your window helps sustain the farmland ahead.

Mikawa-Anjo – Nagoya

Aizuma River

From Mikawa-Anjo toward Nagoya, the train crosses the Aizuma River at Kariya, then the Sakai River. Toward Tokyo, the order is reversed. Watch for the two crossings as you pass through Kariya.

11

Nagoya – Gifu-Hashima

Shonai RiverThe bridges at Biwajima

Shortly after Nagoya toward Shin-Osaka, look beyond the tracks in the foreground for a row of bridge piers. This is Biwajima, where a road bridge, a conventional railway bridge and the Shinkansen bridge cross the same stretch of river.

12

Nagoya – Gifu-Hashima

Kiso River

A wide sheet of water opens up between wooded banks. The Nagara and Ibi belong to the same river system, yet each is a river in its own right. Heading toward Shin-Osaka, you cross the Kiso first, then the Nagara, then the Ibi.

13

Gifu-Hashima – Maibara E-seat side

Nagara RiverHashima Bridge

A row of red triangles marks Hashima Bridge just beside the train. The grey steel flashing past in front belongs to the bridge you are riding over. For a moment the two structures overlap in the window.

14

Gifu-Hashima – Maibara E-seat side

Ibi River

The red Ogaki Bridge is farther away than the bridge over the Nagara. Just right of center, Mount Kinka rises behind it, with Gifu Castle on its summit. The keep is scheduled to be covered with sheeting from late August 2026 to mid-April 2027.

Look for Gifu Castle from the train window →
15

Maibara – Kyoto A-seat side

Echi River

In Shiga, the Echi threads its way between pale gravel bars. Across the water, a Japan Pile sign rises above the trees.

Joyson’s plant by the Echi River →
16

Maibara – Kyoto A-seat side

Yasu River

Look out on the A-seat side for Mount Mikami's triangular outline. Locals call it Omi Fuji. In this view, a bridge is taking shape on the floodplain below the mountain.

Qualitech Pharma by the Yasu River →
17

Maibara – Kyoto E-seat side

Seta RiverSeta no Karahashi Bridge

The Seta is Lake Biwa's only natural outlet. On the E-seat side stands Seta no Karahashi, a bridge on a crossing travelers have used for centuries. Here you can watch the water that leaves the lake flow past the town.

Discover Seta no Karahashi Bridge →
18

Maibara – Kyoto

Kamo RiverJust east of Kyoto Station

Look up from the narrow stream in its gravel bed to a bridge carrying cars, then to a second bridge and the Kyoto skyline. You see this just before arriving at Kyoto from Tokyo, or just after leaving Kyoto for Tokyo.

See Kyoto Station and Kyoto Tower →
19

Kyoto – Shin-Osaka

Katsura River

The water divides around a patch of green, with Kyoto spreading out beyond. Farther downstream, the Katsura meets the Uji and Kizu to form the Yodo River. Follow the water on a map to see how these places connect.

Traveling toward Tokyo? Read the crossings in reverse order. In ordinary cars, the E-seat side is on the right toward Shin-Osaka and on the left toward Tokyo. The A-seat side is opposite.
